# Changelog

What each wave of work added, newest first. The guides under `docs/` describe how the library behaves now; this file is the dated record.

## Wave 32 (2026-10-11)

Frames and offscreen rendering. `webContents.frames(options)` streams a page's rendered frames as `WebContents.Frame` values, a BGRA `Image.Bitmap` and the changed rectangle. `BrowserWindow.make({ offscreen: true })` renders a page offscreen and reports each paint; any other page is observed through Electron's frame subscription, shared between observers. Each observer keeps the newest two frames, so a slow consumer skips frames instead of failing. `painting`, `setPainting`, `setFrameRate` and `invalidate` control an offscreen page. `removeHistoryEntry(index)` removes one history entry other than the active one.

## Wave 31 (2026-10-11)

Application members. `App.configureHostResolver` over `Application.HostResolver`, `App.fileIcon`, `App.applicationNameForProtocol`, `App.userAgentFallback` with `App.setUserAgentFallback`, and the `App.gpuInfoUpdates` stream. `Decidables.App.ClientCertificate` chooses the client certificate a server is answered with: the store's first by default, as Electron does, one by fingerprint, or none, and none when a decider fails. `CommandLine.has` and `CommandLine.value` read the process's switches, `GlobalShortcut.setSuspended` and `suspended` pause every shortcut, and `Screen.dipToScreenPoint` and `screenToDipPoint` convert points on scaled displays.

## Wave 30 (2026-10-11)

Window members. Content geometry (`setContentBounds`, `setContentSize`), `moveAbove`, `setHasShadow` and `shadow`, `setAccessibleTitle` and `accessibleTitle`, the `normal`, `menuBarAutoHide` and `nativeHandle` reads, and, on Linux and Windows, `removeMenu`, `setShape` and `setTitleBarOverlay` over `WindowControls.TitleBarOverlay`. `window.events` streams `WindowControls.Event` values: `ready-to-show`, `app-command` and `system-context-menu`.

## Wave 29 (2026-10-11)

Session data and settings. `Session.clearData` clears browsing data by type and origin over `SessionStorage.ClearData`. Shared compression dictionaries are listed with `sharedDictionaryUsage` and `sharedDictionaries` and cleared with `clearSharedDictionaries`. `spellCheckerDictionaries` streams dictionary downloads, and `setSpellCheckerDictionaryDownloadUrl` moves them to your own server. `extensions` lists a session's extensions and `extensionEvents` streams them loading, becoming ready and unloading. `allowNtlmCredentialsForDomains` and `setCodeCachePath` complete the settings. `Decidables.Session.FileSystemAccess` answers a page's request for a path Chromium treats as sensitive with `allow`, `tryAgain` or `deny`, and denies without an answer.

## Wave 28 (2026-10-11)

Page members on every web contents. `webContents.events` streams `WebContents.PageEvent` values: context-menu requests with what was under the pointer, cursor and link-target changes, the theme color, HTML fullscreen, DOM readiness, preload failures, frames created, navigated and loaded with their process and routing ids, and DevTools opening, closing and taking focus. `activity` and `devTools` are one-shot reads. `toggleDevTools`, `setDevToolsTitle`, `inspectElement` and `inspectWorker` drive DevTools; `enableDeviceEmulation` and `disableDeviceEmulation` emulate a device's screen; `edit(command)`, `replace`, `replaceMisspelling`, `adjustSelection` and `copyImageAt` run editing commands; `setVisualZoomLimits`, `setImageAnimationPolicy` and `takeHeapSnapshot` complete the set. These live in `internal/ContentsPage.ts`, apart from the navigation state and decision gates.

## Wave 27 (2026-10-11)

Device choosers. A page asking for a HID device, a USB device, a serial port or one of several WebAuthn credentials is answered by a decider on the session: `Decidables.Session.HidDevice`, `.UsbDevice`, `.SerialPort` and `.WebAuthnAccount` are asked with the entries the request offers and answer `{ kind: "select", id }` or `{ kind: "cancel" }`, over the schemas in the new `Device` module. `Decidables.Session.BluetoothPairing` answers a pairing prompt on Linux and Windows. `Decidables.WebContents.BluetoothDevice` chooses the device for `navigator.bluetooth.requestDevice` on a window's contents or a guest: it is asked again each time the scan reports devices, a newer report interrupts a decider still deciding on the older list, and `{ kind: "wait" }` keeps scanning. Every native listener exists only while its decidable has a decider, an id the request did not offer cancels it, and a failing decider cancels. `Session.deviceChanges` streams devices that appear, go away or are revoked, and `Session.setUsbProtectedClasses` sets which USB classes pages cannot reach.

## Wave 26 (2026-10-10)

Member coverage on surfaces that already existed, measured against Electron's typings and limited to what runs on every platform. Sessions gained `clearAuthCache`, `clearHostResolverCache`, `clearCodeCaches`, `flushStorage`, `storagePath`, `forceReloadProxyConfig`, the custom spell checker dictionary (`spellCheckerWords`, `addSpellCheckerWord`, `removeSpellCheckerWord`), network emulation (`setNetworkEmulation`, `clearNetworkEmulation` over `Net.Emulation`) and `setSSLConfig` over `Net.SSLConfig`. Windows gained size limits (`setMinimumSize`, `setMaximumSize`, `sizeLimits` over `WindowControls.Size`), `setAspectRatio`, `setEnabled` and `enabled`, `setIgnoreMouseEvents`, `setAutoHideMenuBar`, `setVisibleOnAllWorkspaces` and its read, `setBackgroundColor` and its read, `center` and `moveTop`. Web contents gained `insertText`, `savePage`, `setBackgroundThrottling`, `setIgnoreMenuShortcuts`, and, on owned documents, `reloadIgnoringCache` and `stopLoading`. `App` gained `metrics`, `hasSingleInstanceLock` and `relaunch`.

`Download.Offer` now carries `urlChain`, `userGesture` and `contentDisposition`, so a download decider can tell a download the user asked for from one a page started, and see the redirects behind it. This is a breaking change for code that builds an offer by hand, such as a test.

## Wave 25 (2026-10-10)

Lifetimes, budgets and boundaries hardened, and the toolchain moved to stable releases. Each owner, and each client connection, holds a 128 MiB pending-byte budget through reservations that are released once; an exhausted owner answers `Limit` with `reason: "transport/pending-bytes-limit"` before the handler runs. Request ids are keyed as written, and naming an active request by its other wire type (`"1"` for `1`) is a protocol violation: such a reply fails that call with `Protocol`, and such a request or control message ends the connection. A request's payload decode runs in its own fiber, so a suspended decoder no longer holds the other requests on its connection. In `@sewed/react`, losing the runtime interrupts actions, resources, streams and plain `run` calls, waits for their finalizers and reports `Closed`. A download decider governs with no `downloads` subscriber, and the clipboard and cookie policies validate what they are handed. Number fields across the built-in protocols are `Schema.Finite`, so `NaN` and the infinities are refused at the boundary, and tagged envelopes are `Schema.TaggedStruct`. Effect moved from `4.0.0-rc.115` to `4.0.2`, where the RPC, HTTP and DevTools modules are `effect/rpc`, `effect/http` and `effect/devtools`. Electron moved to `44.7.0` and has not been probed natively there. `tsc` is patched by `@effect/tsgo` and reports Effect diagnostics, `pnpm check:consumer` installs the packed tarballs into a scaffolded app, and the API reference is tokenized by the compiler's own scanner.

Binary stream framing now measures each value once and encodes each bounded fragment once, instead of repeatedly encoding growing prefixes. Frame limits still include MessagePack's fixarray, array16 and array32 headers. `autoresearch.sh` benchmarks public JSON and binary RPCs across real workers, checking byte limits, ordering, typed failures and shutdown.

## Wave 24 (2026-09-24)

Every string arrives as sent on the binary wire, the client's refusal of a host's answer says the host ran, and a test can corrupt one message on purpose. A string holding a lone surrogate, which `JSON.parse` accepts and UTF-8 cannot carry, failed its call when short (msgpackr's own loop writes `ED A0 80`, which the receiver decodes as U+FFFD and the canonical check refuses) and turned silently into U+FFFD when 64 code units or longer (the UTF-8 encoder replaces it); each message is now walked once with `isWellFormed`, allocating nothing, and only a message holding such a string, value or map key, packs a copy that carries it as a msgpackr extension (type `0x75`) of its UTF-16 code units, which decodes to the identical string and keeps the canonical check. The walk costs under 0.1 ms on a 12 MiB byte request and about 2.4 ms on a 13 MiB answer of 63,000 small rows, about 30% of packing it and 7% of decoding it, stated in `docs/binary-streams.md`. The client's own schema refusing an answer or a stream value now carries `reason: "transport/answer-refused"`, decided by re-encoding the payload with the client's codec; a refused typed error claims nothing, since the transport's own typed failure is refused at the same place on an operation that does not admit `SewedError`. `Testing.client` and `Testing.layer` take `tamper: { answer, request }`, predicates over an operation's tag that rewrite a selected answer or request into a packet that parses to the same value but is not its one encoding (on the JSON protocol, one that fails its envelope), so the call fails `transport/undecodable-answer` or `transport/undecodable-request` and the rest continue. The playground's `verify:binary` carries short and long lone-surrogate strings in an answer and a bulk answer to a renderer, and the answer over the worker, utility and relay bindings, red against the previous pack first. The suite went from 846 tests to 854.

## Wave 23 (2026-09-24)

JSON-shaped data round-trips on the binary wire, one message that fails to decode fails the one call it names, and a large Binary message no longer leaves either process holding a packer arena several times its size. msgpackr 2.1.0 renames a decoded `"__proto__"` key to `"__proto_"`, so an answer `main` got from `JSON.parse` failed `decodeBinary`'s canonical check and ended the connection; the decoder keeps its fast path and, only when that check fails, decodes the same bytes again with maps kept as `Map`s and rebuilds each as a plain object whose keys are own properties defined with `Object.defineProperty`, `"__proto__"` included and no prototype set, refusing a map keyed by anything but a string. A packet that parses and names a request, but is not canonical or does not match its envelope, now fails that request alone: an answer or a stream value with `Protocol` and `reason: "transport/undecodable-answer"`, the client telling the owner to stop the request as an interruption would, and a request with `Protocol` and `reason: "transport/undecodable-request"`, its handler not run; an assembled bulk request or bulk answer that does not decode fails its own request. A packet that does not parse, names nothing tracked, or is a bulk part or a part's acknowledgment still ends the connection, and main's renderer bridge and a worker's dependency relay now forward an attributable packet instead of closing on it. msgpackr's arena is module state shared by every `Packr` and grows to four times a message of up to 16 MiB, so one 12 MiB message had left a 48 MiB arena behind for the session; every pack now starts at the front of the arena, a message over 1 MiB keeps the arena it was written to as its own bytes with no further copy, the canonical check compares in place, and a large decode no longer leaves its packet reachable from the decoder. Measured in Node with the client and the owner in two processes, a bulk message of N bytes now peaks at about 3×N on the sender and 4×N on the receiver (12 MiB bulk answer: 4.1× → 2.1× on the sender, 7.0× → 3.9× on the receiver), stated in `docs/binary-streams.md`. The playground's `verify:binary` carries `JSON.parse('{"__proto__":{"x":1},"a":2}')` in an answer and a bulk answer beside a concurrent call on the same connection, red against the previous pack first. The suite went from 829 tests to 845.

## Wave 22 (2026-09-24)

An answer over the envelope fails typed rather than as a defect, a unary operation's own answer can be declared bulk symmetrically with a bulk request, and `keep` holds a window's native title. An owner's `send` used to let `encode`'s synchronous `Limit` throw reach Effect's own RpcServer, which turned it into a per-request `Die` after the handler had already run; `send` now catches that throw for an `Exit` (and, for a stream, for a single value too large to fit even alone) and substitutes the same `failExit` wire shape a lost connection answers with, so the caller sees a typed `SewedError { code: "Unknown", reason: "transport/answer-over-limit" }` through the operation's own error channel — the host ran, its answer did not arrive, and nothing retries it — instead of a bare defect; the same substitution now also backs `Binary.bulkAnswer`'s own over-bound refusal. `Binary.bulkAnswer(rpc, { maxBytes })` declares that a unary operation's own answer, not just its request, may exceed the envelope, refused outside a `Binary.protocol` exactly where `Binary.bulk` is and, new here, on a stream-shaped operation at the declaration itself, since a stream already fragments its own values between messages. The owner encodes its `Exit` against the declared bound instead of the plain envelope limit and, only when the result still does not fit one message, slices the encoded packet into acknowledged `AnswerPart` frames — one in flight, mirroring `RequestPart` in the other direction, reusing the `Ack` mechanism a stream's chunks already wait on — so an answer that happens to be small costs nothing beyond the check; the client intercepts `AnswerPart` in its own connection subscriber, assembles up to the bound plus one envelope, and feeds the reassembled `Exit` into its ordinary inbox, indistinguishable from one that arrived whole. Building it surfaced a second gap beside the one it was meant to close: the owner's `disconnect()` never released a client's pending stream-chunk acknowledgment either, a wait that lives inside `send`'s own fiber rather than the handler's — harmless for a stream, whose still-running handler fiber is what RpcServer's own per-request interruption actually reaches, but fatal for a unary bulk answer, whose handler has already finished by the time its own fragmenting starts, so nothing ever interrupted the wait and a connection lost mid-answer hung the owner forever; `disconnect()` now resolves every pending acknowledgment itself, after closing and unregistering the connection, so the released `send` writes nothing more (as first shipped it released them before the close, and an owner scope closing mid-answer hung on one more part). Electron holds a real window's native title bar only through the BROWSER WINDOW's own `page-title-updated` — probed directly on 44.3.0, a fixed `setTitle` title survives a titled page's load when that event's own `preventDefault` runs and does not when only the contents' event of the same name does, confirming the contents-level `preventDefault` `internal/WebContentsCore.ts` called on `"keep"` was always inert. `internal/Window.ts`'s window-level listener never resolved `Decidables.WebContents.TitleUpdate` at all before this wave; `WindowTitle.holdsTitle(registry, title)`, isolated into its own Electron-free module the same way `WindowClose.ts` is — `internal/Window.ts` itself cannot be imported outside a real Electron process — is what it now calls, against the contents' own decision registry, newly exposed from `internal/WebContents.ts` for this one caller. The title _stream_ is unchanged: it keeps following the page regardless of the decision, as it has since wave 21. The playground's `verify:binary` proves a ~12 MiB `Binary.bulkAnswer` JSON result (63,000 items) arrives whole over the real `contextBridge`/`MessagePort`/`MainBridge` path; `verify:webcontents-extras` proves a fixed native title held under a `keep` decider and followed the page under `accept`, each run RED against the previous pack before the fix landed. The review's fix round closed three blocking defects and the rest of its findings: an oversized stream value now interrupts its parked handler after the typed failure, returning its finalizers and its concurrency permit (the 33rd such call used to never start); a caller interrupting a `Binary.bulkAnswer` call mid-parts ends only that call, the owner stopping after the part in flight and the client dropping that part unacknowledged instead of failing the shared connection; the client applies the 16 KiB floor to a non-final answer part and keeps its assembly on the request's own record; a hostile-part refusal is kept as the `cause` of the `Unknown` answers, and `failExit` sends a cause as its encoded fields, since an error instance there had failed the caller's decode with `Expected JSON value` (the `Limit` cause too, since wave 20); a bulk answer's request ends in diagnostics after its last part; a window's own contents no longer ask `TitleUpdate` a second time for the change the window's listener asked about; and a shared client's build runs in its own scope, so a first caller interrupted mid-connect (a React component unmounting) no longer leaves every later call on the slot waiting forever. The suite went from 807 tests to 816, then 829 with the fix round.

## Wave 21 (2026-09-23)

A web-shaped protocol handler's request stays live until its own body is read, a guest's title stream follows the page whatever the native title decision answers, a testing stand-in's request-observer subscription can be awaited instead of raced, and a bulk request's own last part settles its `sent` flag before a lost connection can answer for it. `Protocol.handleWeb`'s `serveWeb` used to abort `request.signal` the instant a handler's own promise resolved, before Electron or a caller ever read the `Response` body: a proxy handler chaining `fetch(upstream, { signal: request.signal })`, exactly the shape the guide recommends, had that upstream call cancelled mid-transfer on every request, matching a consumer's own probe (`AbortError`, reproduced red first over the fake-session harness and, separately, under real Electron in the playground with a hand-rolled multi-chunk stream: `net::ERR_FAILED` on the unfixed build, 393216 bytes across six chunks read intact, checksum verified, once fixed). A later review refuted a claim recorded here at the time — that a `handleWeb` handler chaining a nested `fetch`/`session.fetch` call crashes this Electron build: probed directly over a 512 KiB local HTTP sidecar, global `fetch`, `net.fetch`, `session.fetch`, and the consumer's own `fetch(new Request(target, request))` shape all read the body whole through `handleWeb`, raw and wrapped, every time — nothing crashes. What had actually failed was the unfixed build's own entry document, itself proxied through `handleWeb` the same way, aborted before a page ever existed to reach any nested `fetch` at all. The playground gained the fetch-shaped route the original ask was for, beside the hand-rolled one that stays: a `/fetch-proxy` `handleWeb` handler chaining `fetch(upstream, { signal: request.signal })` against a real local server streaming 512 KiB over eight chunks, read whole (524288 bytes, checksum verified). The request now finishes without aborting on a body-less response or once a wrapped body reaches `done`; it still aborts on a native abort, the registration's own scope closing in flight, a handler throw, or the page cancelling the body it was handed. That same review found `close()`'s own teardown left an unhandled rejection behind on the cancel path: `controller.abort(reason)` ran before `reader.cancel(reason)`, so a handler chaining `fetch(upstream, { signal: request.signal })` had its own body reject first, and the bare `void` on the following cancel dropped that rejection uncaught — every page cancelling such a proxied body leaked one. `reader.cancel(reason).catch(() => {})` now consumes it, proven red first (a plain-Node pin over the fake session, and a new fourth `protocol.test.ts` Z1 test chaining a real `fetch` to a local server, both showing the unhandled rejection before the fix), green after. `window.webContents.title` and `guest.webContents.title` now follow `page-title-updated` whatever `Decidables.WebContents.TitleUpdate` answers — the decision governs only the native title-bar effect, inert for a guest regardless — where a `"keep"` answer previously suppressed the internal stream too, so a guest needed an `accept` decider installed just to read a title nothing native was ever going to show. `Testing.requests().observe(filter)` is now `Effect<Stream<...>, SewedError, MainContext | Scope.Scope>` rather than a plain `Stream`: its tap is registered by the time the Effect succeeds, closing the asynchronous-registration race every one of its own tests used to paper over with a bounded retry loop; the same review found its release step dropped the tap but left the queue open, so a reader still running on the answered `Stream` hung on a scope close instead of completing — `Queue.endUnsafe(queue)` now runs beside `taps.delete(tap)`, the same shape `internal/Observers.ts`'s own per-subscriber queues already use, proven by joining the forked reader fiber with a bounded timeout (red: timed out; green: joined). And `internal/Transport.ts`'s bulk `fragment` now flips a request's `sent` flag, and clears it from `undelivered`, inline in the same callback its own final part's `connection.send` runs in, rather than downstream of the `Effect.raceFirst` a bulk send races against a lost-connection `Deferred` — probed live over `pair()`: even a synchronous `Effect.try`'s own result reaches a `yield*`-ing generator through the fiber's ordinary resume path, not inline, so a connection closed the instant a withheld acknowledgment's release drove the last part onto the wire answered `Closed`/`transport/closed-before-send` for a request whose bytes had already, provably, left, under the version this replaces. The suite went from 800 tests to 806, then 807 once the review round's own fourth Z1 test landed.

## Wave 18 (2026-09-22)
